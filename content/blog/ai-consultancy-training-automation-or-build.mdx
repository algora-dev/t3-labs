## The problem with shopping for "AI help"

Most businesses don't come to us asking for a specific service. They come with a problem — "quoting takes all evening", "we lose enquiries", "nobody can find anything" — and the technology choice is genuinely unclear to them. That's normal. The labels consultancies use (consultancy, implementation, automation, training, custom development) describe our internal workflows, not anything a business owner should have to learn before making contact.

But if you're the kind of person who likes to know what you're buying, here's the honest decision guide.

## Choose consultancy when you don't know where to begin

**The pattern:** you're fairly sure AI could help your business somewhere, but you can't point at a specific process and say "automate that".

[AI consultancy](/ai-consultancy) means someone looks at how your business actually works and identifies the worthwhile opportunities — and tells you which ones aren't worth doing. The output is a prioritised plan, not software. It suits businesses that want a straight answer before spending build money.

## Choose implementation when you already know what you want

**The pattern:** you have a clear idea of the outcome — "I want AI to read these documents and put the data in my system" — and you need someone to make it work.

[AI implementation](/ai-implementation) covers integrations, APIs, data, workflow design, testing, deployment and handover. It suits businesses that have done the thinking (or had it done) and need the build.

## Choose automation when the problem is repetitive work

**The pattern:** your team keeps doing the same sequence of steps, over and over, and it's eating hours every week.

[AI automation](/ai-automation) means designing workflows where the routine work runs itself and people only handle exceptions and approvals. Note: some of this doesn't even need AI — plain automation is often the better tool. A good provider will tell you which.

## Choose training when your team needs the capability itself

**The pattern:** the work isn't outsourcable — it happens in your team's heads and hands — but your people aren't getting much out of AI yet.

[AI training](/ai-training) built around your actual work — prompts, checking output, safe handling of company data, repeatable workflows. It suits businesses that want internal capability rather than an external system.

## Choose custom software when nothing fits

**The pattern:** the workflow is genuinely specific to your business, and off-the-shelf tools can't follow it.

Sometimes the answer isn't AI at all — it's a custom application shaped around how you actually work. That's the core of what T3 Labs does; QuoteCore+ exists because generic quoting tools didn't match roofing workflows.
