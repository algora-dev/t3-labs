> T3 Labs develops QuoteCore+, quoting and takeoff software for the roofing trade. This article is written from first-hand experience — including our own use of AI in the quoting process.

If you run a trade or small business, quoting is probably the job that eats your evenings. You measure, you price, you type it up, you send it, and then half the time you hear nothing back. And when someone asks "can AI just write my quotes for me?" the honest answer is: partly, yes — but probably not the way you're imagining.

## Where AI actually helps with quotes today

**Getting from messy input to structured numbers.** This is the strongest use of AI in quoting right now. Instead of a human reading a plan, a site photo, or a scribbled note and manually working out measurements, AI can extract the structure: areas, lengths, quantities. We built this ourselves — [AI Scan Assist in QuoteCore+](https://quote-core.com/features/ai-scan-assist) reads a roof plan and identifies the planes, ridges, hips, valleys and barges, so the roofer verifies rather than measures from scratch. That single step is where most quoting time goes.

**Turning numbers into a written quote.** Once the measurements and prices exist, generating the document — line items, totals, terms, a covering email — is exactly the kind of structured writing AI is good at. Not creative writing; filling a template with the right values in the right places.

**Catching what you missed.** A tired human misses a valley or forgets the scaffold line. A machine doesn't get tired. AI is best used as a second pair of eyes on your own work.

## Where it still needs you

AI does not know your customer, your supplier prices this week, or that the access to that particular roof is awful. It doesn't know the job you actually want to win, or the one you're quietly pricing high because you don't want it. Judgement, local knowledge and relationships stay human — and should.

There's also the trust question. A quote is a promise you'll be held to. You wouldn't send an employee's work out unchecked on their first month; treat AI the same. Verify, then send.

## Your realistic options

Roughly, in order of effort:

1. **Keep doing it manually.** Works fine until the volume of quotes stops you doing the actual work.
2. **General AI tools** — chat assistants, document generators. Cheap, flexible, but nothing is connected: you still carry numbers between tools by hand, and re-check everything.
3. **Quoting software with AI built in.** The AI works directly on your plans and your price list, and the output is a finished quote rather than a draft you retype. This is the category we built [QuoteCore+](https://quote-core.com) into — because it's the version that actually removes the evening work.

You don't have to build any of this yourself. Option 3 is a subscription, not a project.

## The part AI can't replace

Every quote you send is a decision about which work you want, at what margin, for which customers. Software — AI or otherwise — can do the arithmetic and the typing. The strategy of what you quote, and to whom, is still the most valuable thing you bring to your own business.

If quoting is the thing slowing you down, that's a solvable problem — and it's a well-trodden one, so you don't need to guess your way through it.
