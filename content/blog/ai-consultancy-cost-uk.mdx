## Why AI consultancy pricing is hard to compare

Compare two quotes for the same job and you'll often find wildly different numbers. That's not (only) because some consultancies are expensive — it's because "AI consultancy" covers everything from a two-hour advisory call to a months-long assessment-plus-build engagement. The label tells you almost nothing about what you're buying.

What actually determines cost is the shape of the work. Here's what's inside it.

## The cost drivers

**Assessment and discovery.** Someone has to understand how your business actually works before recommending anything. The bigger and more varied your operations, the longer this takes. For a small business it might be days; for a mid-sized one with several departments, weeks.

**Integrations.** The single biggest cost driver in most projects. If the AI needs to talk to your CRM, accounting system, inbox or job-management software, each connection is engineering work. One integration is cheap; five is a project.

**Data preparation.** AI is only as good as the information it can reach. Getting your data out of spreadsheets, inboxes and people's heads into a shape a system can read is frequently more work than the AI part itself.

**Automation complexity.** A workflow with three steps and one human checkpoint is much cheaper than one with branching logic, exceptions and multiple approval levels. Complexity scales non-linearly.

**Number of systems involved.** Related to integrations, but broader: every system the work touches adds configuration, permissions, testing and failure modes.

**Testing.** Testing AI-assisted workflows against real cases — not demos — is unavoidable and takes real time. Cutting this is how projects end up "working" in the demo and failing in week two.

**Training and handover.** If your team needs to run the result, budget for training on the actual system. If you want your team independent rather than dependent, handover is a deliberate piece of work.

**Ongoing support.** Systems drift: tools change, edge cases appear, your business evolves. Some engagements include a support retainer; some are build-and-hand-over. Know which you're buying.

**Security and compliance needs.** If you handle sensitive personal, financial or health-related data, additional safeguards, access controls and review steps add cost — and are not optional.

## Engagement models

Broadly, three shapes exist in the UK market:

**Advisory-only.** You pay for assessment and recommendations — a plan you can take to any provider (or act on yourself). Cost scales with the size of the business and depth of the assessment.

**Assessment plus implementation.** The common shape: the consultant assesses, then builds. Typically quoted per project once scope is clear, with the assessment often credited against the build.

**Retainer.** Ongoing availability — monitoring, refining, extending. Suits businesses whose workflows keep evolving, or who want AI capability available without hiring.

## Cheap versus expensive

A suspiciously cheap quote usually means one of: templated advice not specific to your business, the expensive parts (integrations, data, testing) skipped or discovered later, or work offshored with thin oversight — which can be fine, or not. An expensive quote should be justifiable line by line against the drivers above. If a provider can't explain what drives their number, that itself is information.
